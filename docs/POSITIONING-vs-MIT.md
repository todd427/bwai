## <span style="color:#16A085">1. One-line position</span>

BWAI is the engineer-grade, ship-something-real course for individuals. MIT Professional Education sells credential-and-prestige AI courses to employers. **They occupy different markets and barely compete.** MIT is a useful *comparator* that sharpens BWAI's positioning by contrast — not a threat.

## <span style="color:#16A085">4. Strategic read</span>

**They don't compete.** Nobody finishes an MIT Prof Ed course having shipped a deployed agentic tool from a CLI. BWAI's buyer is the individual chasing employability; MIT's is the L&D department buying a signal.

**The market is being warmed above and below BWAI's lane, not in it.** AIReady (state-funded, ~100 min, citizen literacy) sets day-one for citizens. MIT's "No Code and Agentic AI" confirms *agentic* is now a sellable headline — pitched no-code, which leaves the engineer-grade agentic slot exactly where BWAI already sits. Same gap-confirmation AIReady gave from the other side.

**What MIT has that can't be bought:** certificate signalling. Don't try to out-brand MIT — impossible. But MIT can't out-*authenticate* BWAI: a course taught by someone who built the platform you learn on, who shipped a production system solo, is a proof-of-method the adjunct-plus-partner model structurally can't produce.

**The real competitor remains Anthropic Academy** (free, authoritative, learn-the-tool). The answer to Anthropic is also the answer to MIT: *ship something real* beats both *learn-the-tool* and *earn-a-cert*.
